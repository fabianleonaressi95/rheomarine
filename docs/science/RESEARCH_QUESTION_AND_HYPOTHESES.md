# RheoMarine-SLO — research question and falsifiable hypotheses

## Central research question

To what extent can the spatial and temporal variability of floating microplastics in the Slovenian coastal sea be explained by observed surface circulation, and to what extent does introducing particle-specific effective properties — including size, density, shape, biological conditioning and aggregation — improve the representation of their transport and loss from the surface layer?

A second, conditional question is whether rheological effects measured in appropriately characterized laboratory media add explanatory or predictive value beyond particle-specific settling and aggregation. This is not assumed in advance.

## Hypotheses

**H1 — Surface hydrodynamic control.** Surface circulation contributes measurably to the spatial and temporal redistribution of floating particles. A model forced by quality-controlled HFR currents should reproduce a component of observed transport or concentration variability, within the limits of the available independent observations.

**H2 — Particle-specific behaviour.** At matched hydrodynamic forcing, particle classes with different size, density and shape exhibit different horizontal pathways, surface-layer residence and modelled settling/loss. A particle-resolved configuration may improve selected predictions relative to a passive-tracer baseline.

**H3 — Biological conditioning and aggregation.** Biofouling and aggregation can alter effective particle properties and settling behaviour. Their inclusion is warranted only where rates or parameter ranges are supported by measured data or traceable literature; density change and aggregate formation must be treated as distinct processes.

**H4 — Conditional rheological contribution.** Rheological properties of a particle–medium system may affect settling under specific conditions. A rheological correction is retained only if measured constitutive behaviour is relevant to the experimental conditions and produces a robust, identifiable effect. Ordinary seawater is not assumed to be non-Newtonian, and xanthan-gum/carrageenan solutions are treated as laboratory proxies, not natural EPS.

## Falsifiability and interpretation

- H1 is not supported if surface-current-driven simulations fail to explain observed variability beyond uncertainty, subject to adequate HFR coverage and compatible observations.
- H2 is not supported if adding particle properties does not improve predeclared metrics or process diagnostics beyond uncertainty and reasonable parameter variation.
- H3 is not supported if aggregation/biofouling parameters are unavailable, unidentifiable or do not produce a robust effect.
- H4 is not supported if measured rheological properties are absent, irrelevant to the tested regime, or fail the M4 go/no-go criteria.

A failure to demonstrate an effect is a valid result. The project does not presuppose that M1 or M2 must outperform M0.

## Model hierarchy

- **M0:** surface advection–diffusion baseline forced by QC-controlled HFR, with documented conventional settling/loss assumptions.
- **M1:** M0 plus evidence-supported particle classes and effective-property/aggregation processes.
- **M2:** conditional rheological correction, activated only after the M4 evidence gate.

## Scope

The project concerns surface transport and a parameterized loss from a defined surface layer. HFR does not directly measure vertical circulation or seabed deposition. Field validation requires independent observations compatible with the model output. Historical Slovenian observations are regional benchmarks unless sample-level metadata and temporal/spatial compatibility are established.

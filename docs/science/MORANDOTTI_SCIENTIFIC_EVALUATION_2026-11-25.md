# Scientific evaluation — Marco Morandotti (micro/nanomotors)

**Date:** 25 November 2026
**Purpose:** assess the scientific profile of Marco Morandotti (Politecnico
di Torino) and his relevance to RheoMarine-SLO, based on publicly available
sources (institutional pages, CV, publication metadata). This is a
bibliometric/profile assessment, not a peer review of his papers.

## Profile (verified)

  `staff.polito.it/marco.morandotti`).

  SISSA (2014–2016), ICTI Carnegie Mellon / IST Lisbon (2011–2014).
- PhD in Applied Mathematics, SISSA, 2011: *Self propulsion in viscous
  fluids through shape deformation* (advisors G. Dal Maso, A. DeSimone).

- Core research area: **low-Reynolds-number locomotion of micro-organisms
  and artificial micro/nanomotors in viscous fluids**, geometric control
  theory, fluid–structure interaction, particulate transport.

## Scientific assessment

**Strengths**

- Rigorous mathematical treatment of low-Reynolds-number swimming:
  existence/uniqueness, controllability (geometric control), optimal
  control, kinetic limits.
- Sustained productivity and coherent research programme built around
  the SISSA/DeSimone school of swimmer hydrodynamics.
- The Brinkman-fluid work (2012) is directly adjacent to transport in
  complex/porous media — relevant to EPS-rich or aggregated
  microplastic-laden water where effective-medium descriptions appear.
- Recent work on *passive* particles advected by active ones and on
  spherical-particle ensembles in viscous fluids (JFM 2025,
  arXiv:2410.16793) is the closest bridge to particle-transport
  modelling.

**Limits for RheoMarine-SLO**

- His objects are **active, self-propelled** swimmers/motors. RheoMarine
  concerns **passive** microplastic particles settling and advecting.
  The physics overlaps (low-Re hydrodynamics, drag, Brownian/rotational
  diffusion) but the research questions differ: he studies
  controllability of locomotion, we study settling velocity
  retardation and large-scale transport.
- No evident work on **non-Newtonian bulk rheology** (shear-thinning,
  yield stress, EPS gels) as a transport medium — his fluids are
  Newtonian viscous or Brinkman-type effective media.
- No evident work on **settling** per se; his particles are neutrally
  buoyant swimmers, not density-stratified sinkers.
- HFR/oceanographic data, field validation, or environmental
  microplastics are outside his apparent scope.

**Relevance verdict:** scientifically strong profile in micro-scale
hydrodynamics and control; **moderate-to-low direct relevance** to the
RheoMarine-SLO core (passive microplastic settling in non-Newtonian
seawater forced by HFR surface currents). Plausible value as a
discussion partner for low-Re particle–fluid interaction theory,
Brinkman-type effective-medium arguments, or possible secondment/
collaboration on particle-ensemble modelling — not as a domain
co-investigator for the oceanographic/rheological core.

## Suggested use

- If outreach is pursued, frame around: particle–fluid interactions at
  low Re, Brinkman/porous-media descriptions of structured seawater,
  and kinetic/mean-field limits for particle ensembles — not around
  micro-nanomotor applications.
- Any claimed collaboration must remain unclaimed until confirmed;
  this evaluation does not imply contact, interest, or partnership.

# RISTANC — ethical and open-science review for RheoMarine-SLO

Date: 25 November 2026  
Primary source: official RISTANC Ethical Aspects, version 2 (28 September 2026), 5 pages.  
Official source: https://www.ristanc.eu/storage/doc/202609/04ethical-aspectsristancv5.pdf

## 1. What the document requires

The RISTANC Standard Ethical Protocol was approved by the Scientific Council of ZRS Koper. Funded projects are expected to apply its principles in a context-appropriate way. The document also states that funded projects will be asked to sign the included Informed Consent form at contract signature.

The principles include:
- respect for human dignity and integrity;
- honesty and transparency, including disclosure of actual and perceived conflicts of interest;
- transparent use of AI with human oversight;
- voluntary participation and free, informed consent where people participate;
- protection of vulnerable people;
- privacy, confidentiality and GDPR-compliant data processing;
- justice and inclusion;
- minimising harm and maximising benefit;
- environmental protection and care for future generations;
- open science.

Posts and publications generated during the project must be freely accessible. Data must be shared under “as open as possible, as closed as necessary”.

## 2. Important distinction: applicant consent vs. project research participants

The included consent form is framed around participation in the RISTANC transdisciplinary research process. It explains that the applicant/participant may be asked to contribute as a stakeholder with knowledge and views on the sea and its management. The form is to be signed at contract signature and includes a choice whether the participant is referred to by real name, chosen pseudonym or a researcher-assigned title.

The form also states:
- participation is voluntary and can be withdrawn without reason or consequences;
- anonymised data may be used for research, publications, reports and repositories;
- photographs may be published only with the relevant consent selection;
- primary data are accessible to the ZRS Koper research group during the project and up to five years after its end;
- results may be made public, primarily through the RISTANC website.

This RISTANC process consent should not be confused with separate consent/information duties if RheoMarine itself interviews stakeholders, records meetings, collects identifiable personal data or takes photographs. If no human participants are recruited and only environmental/laboratory data are used, state that accurately; do not invent a human-subjects work package.

## 3. Project-specific ethical and environmental safeguards

### Laboratory containment and environmental protection
- No experimental microplastic particles, xanthan, carrageenan or other polymer dispersions are to be released into the sea, drains or natural environment.
- Conduct particle and polymer tests in contained laboratory systems.
- Collect, label and dispose of suspensions, filters, contaminated consumables and residues through the laboratory's approved waste procedures.
- Use the smallest practicable sample volumes and particle quantities.
- Obtain permits and documented sampling consent/authorisation before collecting natural seawater, EPS or biological material in Slovenian waters.
- Do not collect organisms or disturb habitats unless separately authorised and scientifically necessary.
- Record sample origin, date, handling, storage and chain of custody.

### Data protection and stakeholder engagement
- Prefer non-personal environmental data.
- Collect only personal data strictly necessary for project coordination or voluntary stakeholder engagement.
- If interviews, workshops, audio recordings or photographs are added, prepare a short information sheet, purpose, lawful basis, retention period, access controls and withdrawal process before collection.
- Do not publish names, contact details, identifiable quotations or images without an appropriate legal basis and consent.
- Keep stakeholder feedback separate from scientific measurements and clearly label qualitative evidence.

### AI transparency
- Disclose use of AI tools where they materially support project activities, in line with the call's ethical document.
- The applicant remains responsible for scientific claims, data processing, code, interpretation and final outputs.
- Do not upload confidential, personal or restricted third-party data to AI services without an appropriate legal basis and safeguards.
- Maintain human review of AI-assisted text, code and analysis; preserve provenance and correct errors.

### Conflicts and integrity
- Declare actual and perceived conflicts of interest.
- Do not present OGS, a laboratory or an individual as a partner, provider or supporter without confirmation.
- Separate independent validation data from calibration data.
- Preserve negative and inconclusive findings; do not selectively report only favourable model comparisons.
- Cite datasets and papers, respect licences and distinguish measured results from hypotheses.

## 4. Open-science and intellectual-property implementation

- Make project posts and publications freely accessible.
- Publish project-generated data where lawful and technically appropriate, with metadata and clear licences.
- For third-party HFR and Mendeley data, follow the original licence, access conditions and attribution; “open” does not override third-party rights.
- If data cannot be shared, document the reason and provide metadata or an access procedure where possible.
- Version code and analysis workflows; include environment/dependency information and reproducibility instructions.
- Keep pre-existing RheoMarine code, models, methods and datasets clearly identified as background IP in the funding agreement.
- Do not promise open release of third-party or restricted data.

## 5. Suggested text for the application

> The project will use environmental datasets and contained laboratory experiments. No experimental microplastic particles or polymer dispersions will be released into the marine environment. Laboratory suspensions, filters and contaminated consumables will be collected and disposed of through the laboratory's approved procedures. Any collection of natural seawater or biological material will be undertaken only after confirming the applicable permissions and sampling conditions. The project does not currently plan to recruit human research participants; if stakeholder interviews, workshops, recordings or photographs are introduced, appropriate information, consent and data-protection procedures will be established before collection. Personal data will be minimised. AI-assisted tools, if used in project work, will be disclosed transparently and subject to human review. Publications will be made freely accessible, while data sharing will follow the principle “as open as possible, as closed as necessary” and respect third-party licences and confidentiality.

Adapt this wording to the actual activities and the DOCX form; do not submit it unchanged if the project design changes.

## 6. Items to verify before signature

- Exact scope of the RISTANC Informed Consent form and whether the applicant signs as a participant in the programme's own research process.
- Whether the project includes stakeholder interviews or only scientific/technical consultation.
- Any local permissions for natural seawater/EPS sampling.
- Laboratory waste handling and documentation.
- GDPR roles and retention periods if identifiable data are collected.
- Third-party data licences and the boundary between background IP and new results.

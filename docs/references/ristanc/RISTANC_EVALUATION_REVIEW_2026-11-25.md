# RISTANC — evaluation criteria review for RheoMarine-SLO

Date: 25 November 2026  
Primary source: official RISTANC Evaluation Criteria, version 2 (28 September 2026), 3 pages.  
Official source: https://www.ristanc.eu/storage/doc/202609/03evaluationcriteriaristancv5.pdf

## 1. Scoring structure

The panel scores seven criteria for a total of 100 points:

| No. | Criterion | Maximum | What the application must demonstrate |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Consistency with the call challenges; clarity and appropriateness of the main objective; monitoring of achievement | 20 | Direct fit with a Slovenian-sea challenge, a precise objective and measurable indicators |
| 2 | Quality and feasibility of implementation plan and timeline | 20 | Work packages, sequence, responsibilities, milestones, dependencies and realistic duration |
| 3 | Innovation and originality | 10 | What is new in the proposed solution or its local application, without unsupported novelty claims |
| 4 | Expected impact, including target groups | 20 | Concrete environmental, scientific and local stakeholder value; plausible route to use |
| 5 | Sustainability of results | 10 | How outputs, data, code, methods and relationships remain usable after the grant |
| 6 | Applicant competencies and experience in relation to project and call | 10 | Evidence of ability to deliver the modelling/data work and access to missing marine/laboratory competence |
| 7 | Cost-effectiveness of the budget | 10 | Traceable link between costs, work packages, deliverables and expected value |

Each criterion has a stepped scale. For criteria 1, 2 and 4: 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. For criteria 3, 5, 6 and 7: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. The call page states a minimum threshold of 55/100.

## 2. RheoMarine evidence and gaps by criterion

### Criterion 1 — relevance and objective (20)
**Existing strength:** the project addresses microplastic pollution, transport and settling, which fit the broad third challenge. The model comparison M0/M1/conditional M2 gives a testable scientific structure.

**Critical gap:** the application must anchor the project in the Slovenian territorial sea, not simply the North Adriatic. State the selected Slovenian coastal area, HFR cells/stations, data period, local relevance and intended users. Do not claim HFR resolves vertical transport or seabed deposition.

**Required revision:** one sentence objective; 2–4 measurable success indicators; map/description of Slovenian pilot area; explain why results are useful to Slovenian sea management.

### Criterion 2 — feasibility and timeline (20)
**Existing strength:** eight-month WP structure, M0 baseline, biological M1 and evidence-gated M2, independent validation principle and a minimum lab protocol.

**Critical gap:** no confirmed laboratory quote or written capacity; HFR coverage and independent validation data remain to be confirmed. A January 1 start must not be represented as fixed if actual start is only after agreement signature.

**Required revision:** month-relative Gantt; each WP with lead, inputs, output and acceptance test; fallback if OGS/lab access is unavailable; decision gate at month 4 for M2; reserve time for final reporting. Distinguish activities under applicant control from external dependencies.

### Criterion 3 — innovation (10)
**Existing strength:** an integrated, falsifiable comparison of hydrodynamic baseline, biological particle transformation and a conditional rheological settling correction in a defined local setting.

**Risk:** claiming that seawater is generally non-Newtonian, that natural EPS are equivalent to xanthan/carrageenan, or that the rheological correction is already demonstrated would weaken credibility.

**Required revision:** describe innovation as the *test and validation architecture* and its local application, not as a proven new physical law. Explain what decision the comparison enables that M0 alone cannot.

### Criterion 4 — impact and target groups (20)
**Existing strength:** potential relevance to marine litter monitoring, local coastal management, environmental research and future monitoring design.

**Critical gap:** impact cannot be left as “better understanding”. The dossier needs a credible user pathway and named target groups, without implying that any organisation has committed.

**Required revision:** identify likely users (local marine/environmental authorities, monitoring bodies, researchers and coastal stakeholders); specify what they receive (maps/uncertainty-aware model comparison, dataset, reproducible code, concise technical brief); describe a dissemination/feedback meeting only as planned unless confirmed. Quantify deliverables rather than promise unmeasured environmental improvement.

### Criterion 5 — sustainability (10)
**Existing strength:** code/data reproducibility, documented workflows and a reusable M0–M2 comparison.

**Critical gap:** no explicit post-project maintenance or reuse plan.

**Required revision:** state repository location, versioned code, metadata, open licence subject to third-party data terms, long-term storage route, and who can reuse the model. Do not promise permanent maintenance by an institution that has not agreed.

### Criterion 6 — applicant competence (10)
**Existing strength:** computational modelling, Python, numerical analysis, reproducible workflows, published writing and existing research outputs.

**Critical gap:** limited documented oceanographic/laboratory track record. This is a real assessment risk, not something to conceal.

**Required revision:** CV mapped to project tasks; list relevant code/data analyses and publications accurately; explain the limits of the applicant's expertise; document external technical input only if a person/institution confirms it. A non-binding expression of interest is useful evidence but is not a partnership or guarantee of service.

### Criterion 7 — cost-effectiveness (10)
**Existing strength:** current budget arithmetic is internally consistent: A.3 €6,600 + B.1 €3,000 + C €600 + indirect costs €1,800 = €12,000; the 25% indirect calculation on A+C is consistent with the Guidelines.

**Critical gap:** B.1 is only a provisional allocation, not a quote; C €600 needs itemisation; A.3 needs hours/months and unit cost; the laboratory quote must clarify VAT.

**Required revision:** tie every budget line to WP and deliverable; itemise C; state the basis for A.3; obtain a priced laboratory offer or reduce scope transparently; avoid double-counting consumables already included in B.1.

## 3. Scoring discipline

Do not assign a predicted panel score as if it were objective. Use the following internal readiness labels:
- **Documented:** directly evidenced in the application or an attached source.
- **Plausible but unsupported:** reasonable, but needs a source, commitment or measurable detail.
- **Open dependency:** depends on external data, laboratory access, or an agreement.
- **Not claimed:** deliberately excluded to avoid overstatement.

A strong submission should convert as many high-weight criteria (1, 2 and 4; 60 points combined) as possible from plausible/open to documented. A polished scientific method cannot compensate for a weak local impact pathway or an infeasible implementation plan.

## 4. Immediate priority order

1. Lock the Slovenian pilot geography and show its link to the third call challenge.
2. Complete the month-by-month work plan with fallback routes and the month-4 M2 gate.
3. Turn expected impact into specific outputs and users.
4. Add a realistic sustainability/data/code plan.
5. Map CV evidence to each task and acknowledge the missing marine-lab expertise.
6. Obtain the laboratory quote and complete the cost-to-deliverable table.
7. Complete the official application form using the current version and cross-check every declaration.

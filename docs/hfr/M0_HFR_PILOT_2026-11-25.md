## Unit and coordinate contract (verified against `.das` metadata)

- `EWCT`, `NSCT`: **m s⁻¹**, eastward/northward components.
- `latitude`/`longitude`: degrees N/E (EPSG:4326 source).
- `time`: seconds since 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z.
- Projection: local equirectangular, `R = 6371000 m`, centred on the
  subset bbox centre → positions in **metres**. Adequate at ~30 km
  domain scale; not a conformal projection.
- Velocity components need no rotation: east/north align with the local
  x/y axes of this projection.

## QC policy (audited version)

- `valid = (QCflag == 1) & EWCT,NSCT ≠ fill(-32.767) & finite & |v| ≤ 10 m/s`.
  Only flag **1 (good_data)** is accepted.
- Invalid cells keep a `0.0` placeholder masked `valid=False`; never
  interpolated (`VelocityField.sample` raises on invalid stencils).
- **Missing ≠ zero.**

## Trajectory acceptance rules (`simulate_qc_aware`)

A particle step on one observed interval is committed only if ALL hold:

1. start-position stencil valid at **both** bracketing timestamps
   (the engine interpolates velocity between them);
2. advected-endpoint stencil valid at the next timestamp;
3. **pathwise (exact)**: every grid cell touched by the straight
   advected path — computed by exact segment→cell traversal
   (`_cells_on_segment`, conservative DDA over the rectilinear grid,
   including all cells adjacent to exact edge/vertex grazes) — must
   have all four corner nodes QC-valid at both bracketing times.
   No sampling density is involved, so sub-cell corner crossings
   cannot evade the check.

Rejection causes are tracked separately:

- `qc_stopped` — missing QC support; particle keeps its last accepted
  position and never resumes;
- `exited` — removed by the boundary policy (`reflect`/`absorb` on the
  artificial crop window; **not a coastline**).

## Temporal-gap policy

1. Missing timestamps → segments split at gaps > `max_gap_s` (3 h).
2. All-invalid timestamps (zero QC-valid cells) act as barriers.
3. Cell-level QC dropouts → per-step acceptance rules above.

## Pilot design (reproducible)

- Inputs (sha256 verified identical to baseline):
  - IZOL `d37844f5…a1adef5d`, PIRA `908ee53d…4aafc353`.
- Sub-region: 4×4-cell window with highest mean temporal cell validity
  (computational criterion, **not** a geographically representative
  pilot polygon).
- Release: deterministic 8×8 candidate lattice, kept only where the t0
  stencil is fully valid.
- Integration: one step per observed interval; K=0; seed 42.

## Regenerated results (endpoint + pathwise QC)

Inputs identical to baseline; only the acceptance rules changed.

| Site | Run | Longest segment | alive/qc/ex | Active-only median disp. |
|------|-----|-----------------|-------------|--------------------------|
| IZOL | block4 reflect | 70.5 h, 64 particles | 64 / 0 / 0 | 3.34 km |
| IZOL | block8 reflect | 70.5 h, 64 | 64 / 0 / 0 | 7.03 km |
| IZOL | block4 absorb | 70.5 h, 64 | 0 / 0 / **64** | — (all exited) |
| PIRA | block4 reflect | 70.5 h, 64 | 64 / 0 / 0 | 2.56 km |
| PIRA | block8 reflect | 70.5 h, 27 | 0 / **27** / 0 | — (all QC-stopped) |
| PIRA | block4 absorb | 70.5 h, 64 | 0 / 0 / **64** | — (all exited) |

Boundary/domain sensitivity is **large**: under `absorb` essentially the
whole cohort leaves the small crop within days, and the 8×8 window
degrades PIRA coverage enough to stop every particle in the longest
segment. Reflecting-bound results must therefore be read as
closed-box sensitivity experiments, not coastal retention estimates.

PIRA's earlier segments still show QC attrition under the strict rules
(e.g. 47/64 stopped in the first 58.5 h segment), consistent with its
lower coverage. After the exact-traversal upgrade (PR #4 follow-up)
intermediate PIRA segments show slightly higher attrition than the
sampled check (seg 1: 26 alive / 13 stopped vs 30 / 9 before); the
longest-segment headline numbers above are unchanged.

## Interpretation limits

Advection-only, K=0, surface layer, ~30 km domain, artificial crop
boundary. Demonstrates pipeline correctness and QC bookkeeping — not
predictive skill, vertical transport, settling, or seabed deposition.

Pathwise caveats: the exact traversal certifies the *straight*
Euler–Maruyama segment per interval; it assumes the velocity is
constant within the interval (true for the K=0 forward-Euler step),
and under `reflect` a path that would bounce off the artificial
boundary is checked as the straight line to the reflected endpoint —
documented, not exact.
